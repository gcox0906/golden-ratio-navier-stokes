A verified golden-ratio auxiliary covering for the Navier–Stokes construction

I have checked an alternative auxiliary torus covering for OpenAI's existing
Navier–Stokes construction. All credit for the original PDE construction and
formalization remains with the upstream authors:
https://github.com/openai/NavierStokesAndEuler

The change replaces [[3,1],[1,5]] by [[3,1],[1,4]]. Its determinant is 11,
its eigenvalues are (7±sqrt(5))/2, and its eigendirections use
b=(sqrt(5)−1)/2 with b²+b=1. The dependent cover-degree, lattice-quotient,
Fourier-inversion and chart-scale proofs have been updated consistently.

At the pinned upstream base f9e8bc5b38b6e212696e8a30e3e91517af887bbd:

- The full NavierStokes library compiled with warnings treated as errors
(820 source modules).
- The unchanged whole-space and periodic Comparator challenge targets passed
with enable_nanoda=true. Both nanoda and Lean's kernel accepted their
exported dependent proofs; Comparator exited zero.
- Only propext, Quot.sound and Classical.choice were permitted. The challenge
definitions were unchanged, and the final proofs reach the replacement
auxiliary construction.

The contribution reduces the covering degree from 14^d to 11^d and changes
rho from approximately 0.562471 to 0.567283. The original rho already exceeds
1/2. I am not claiming a new threshold, a wider h range, a stronger PDE
statement, universal optimality, or a connection to black-hole physics.

The attached review package contains golden-covering.patch (all 20 construction
modules), the short technical note, the complete build/checker logs and source
inventories, pinned reproduction instructions, and SHA-256 hashes. The checker
used a container-compatible seccomp wrapper in place of the documented
systemd wrapper; the scripts and tested sandbox properties are disclosed.

Would this alternative covering be useful as an upstream option or example?
If so, I would welcome guidance on the preferred abstraction for a general
family of admissible auxiliary matrices.
